Timelines

Two models, drawn as time moving left to right. A Ractor is a Ruby actor with its own threads and objects. Ractors talk through ports: anyone can send to a port, only its owner can receive from it. Each lane below is one Ractor or one thread.

1. A worker pool, one schedule at a time

Main owns a FIFO of three jobs and one result port per worker (R1, R2). Each worker owns its default port D. Main sends jobs, workers process them and send results to main's result ports, main collects them with Ractor.select, runs one global GC, then stops and observes each worker. The three jobs travel in three different ways: job 1 is shared (both sides refer to the same frozen object), job 2 is copied (the worker gets a deep copy), job 3 is moved (main loses it).

2. fork splits the process

Main has two Ractors: main itself, with threads main thread and T1, and a worker with thread T2. T1 and T2 block in receive, main registers an at_exit handler, then forks. The child process keeps only the thread that called fork. The other threads are gone: they do not wake up and no ensure clause runs. The child sees the worker as terminated.
